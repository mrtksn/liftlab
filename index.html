How the model works
  • One rigid body with six degrees of freedom, integrated at 2 kHz. The controller runs at 1 kHz.
  • Motors push along their axis and follow commands with a first-order spin-up lag. Each one also twists the frame by κ × thrust, opposite to its spin.
  • Servos move at a fixed maximum speed within their limit. Tilting changes thrust direction only; the motor's mass stays at the pivot and gyroscopic torque is ignored.
  • A cable is a stiff, tension-only spring with damping. The payload is a point mass that can swing, go slack and hit the ground.
  • Position PID produces a desired force. Attitude uses geometric control with integral action. Gains are set in acceleration units and multiplied by the model's inertia, so they carry over to new geometry.
  • Allocation runs in two stages of bounded weighted least squares. Stage 1 chooses servo angles using virtual inputs (T·cos θ, T·sin θ). Stage 2 solves motor thrusts at the servos' measured angles.
  • The controller only knows about masses and cables marked "Controller knows". Anything else it must absorb with integral action.
  • The envelope is the set of accelerations the actuators can produce (a zonotope), linearized over servo range. It is checked against what hover needs, including static cable loads.
